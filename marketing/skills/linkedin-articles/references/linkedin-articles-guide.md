# LinkedIn Articles Guide

Reference for drafting long-form LinkedIn articles and newsletter-style professional posts. Articles are LinkedIn's durable, SEO-indexed format — distinct from feed posts (ephemeral, ~1,300–3,000 chars). Pull author voice and positioning from repo-local voice docs.

## Article vs. post — when to use which

| | Feed post | Article / newsletter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Length | 1–3 short paragraphs | 800–2,000+ words |
| Lifespan | Hours–days (algorithmic) | Months–years (indexed, searchable) |
| Best for | Quick takes, engagement, reach | Depth, authority, evergreen reference |
| Discovery | Feed algorithm | Search, profile, newsletter subscribers |
| Reach pattern | High initial, fast decay | Lower spike, long tail |

Use articles for: frameworks, in-depth how-tos, contrarian arguments with evidence, case studies, and anything you want to link to later. Newsletters add a subscriber list and email notification — best for a recurring, themed series.

## Structure

1. **Title** — the highest-leverage element. Specific, benefit- or curiosity-driven, ideally with a number or a clear payoff. "What I learned shipping 50 features in a quarter" beats "Thoughts on shipping." Front-load keywords (it's searchable).
2. **Hook (first 2–3 lines)** — LinkedIn shows a preview; earn the click. Open with a tension, a surprising claim, a specific moment, or a question. No throat-clearing.
3. **Promise / framing** — tell the reader what they'll get and why it's worth their time.
4. **Body** — one idea per section with descriptive subheads. Use the structure that fits:
   - *Listicle* ("7 lessons…") — scannable, high completion.
   - *Narrative* (story → insight) — high emotional engagement.
   - *Framework* (here's a model) — high save/share, authority.
   - *Contrarian* (common belief → why it's wrong → better way) — high comment.
5. **Takeaway** — crisp summary or the one thing to remember.
6. **CTA** — invite a comment ("What's your experience?"), a follow, or a subscribe. One ask.

## Formatting for readability

- Short paragraphs (1–3 sentences). Dense blocks kill completion.
- Subheads every few paragraphs — most people skim first.
- Bullets and numbered lists for steps and enumerations.
- Bold sparingly for the key line in a section.
- One relevant image/diagram near the top lifts engagement; add more to break up length.
- Avoid markdown that LinkedIn won't render (it supports basic formatting in the article editor, not raw markdown syntax).

## Length & cadence

- Sweet spot: **800–1,500 words** for most; up to ~2,000 for deep pieces. Long enough for substance, short enough to finish.
- Newsletters: pick a consistent cadence (weekly/biweekly) and theme; consistency drives subscriber growth more than any single piece.

## Engagement patterns

- **Title and hook decide reach** — most readers never get past the preview.
- **Comments > likes** for the algorithm; end with a genuine question and reply to early comments quickly.
- **Saves and shares** signal lasting value — frameworks and how-tos earn these.
- **Cross-promote**: announce the article with a native feed post (don't just drop the link cold — the algorithm suppresses outbound links; lead with value, link in a comment or after the hook).
- **Personal > corporate**: first-person experience, opinions, and specifics outperform polished brand-speak.

## Voice & quality

- Write in the author's real voice — first person, opinionated, specific. Run drafts through the `humanizing` lane to strip AI tells.
- Back claims with concrete numbers, examples, and named experiences. No fabricated stats or anecdotes.
- One clear thesis per article; if you have two, write two articles.
- Professional but human — LinkedIn rewards authenticity over corporate gloss.

## Pre-publish checklist

- [ ] Title is specific and front-loads the payoff/keyword.
- [ ] Hook earns the click in the first 2 lines.
- [ ] One clear thesis; sections each advance it.
- [ ] Subheads make it skimmable; paragraphs are short.
- [ ] Claims are concrete and true.
- [ ] Ends with one clear CTA / question.
- [ ] Companion feed post drafted to drive traffic.
- [ ] Voice matches the author; AI tells removed.
