# Initial Outreach Patterns

Reference for drafting first-touch outreach across email, LinkedIn, X, and direct-response channels. The first touch has one job: earn a reply by being relevant, brief, and easy to say no to. Personalize from real signal; use only approved claims. Outreach is human-gated — draft, don't send.

## First-touch principles

- **Relevance over volume.** One message that's clearly about *them* beats fifty templated ones. Every first touch references something specific and true.
- **Brevity wins.** Shorter messages get more replies. Respect the reader's time; you haven't earned a long pitch yet.
- **Lead with them, not you.** Their world, their pain, their trigger — before your product.
- **One clear, low-friction ask.** Make saying yes (or even no) easy.
- **Value-first.** Give a reason to reply that benefits them (an insight, a relevant resource), not just "can I have 15 minutes."

## Anatomy of a cold message

1. **Opener / personalization** — the specific, true reason you're reaching out to *this* person now (a trigger, a signal, a shared context). Not "I came across your profile."
2. **Relevance bridge** — connect their situation to a problem you understand.
3. **Value / insight** — a concrete reason this matters to them (a result you've driven for similar companies, an observation).
4. **Soft CTA** — a single, low-commitment next step ("worth a quick chat?" / "want me to send the breakdown?").
5. **Easy out** — implicitly or explicitly let them decline without friction.

## Subject lines (email)

The subject decides whether the message is read at all.
- **Short** (2–5 words) — looks personal, renders on mobile.
- **Specific and relevant** — reference their company/trigger, not generic benefit-speak.
- **Curiosity or relevance**, not clickbait — overpromising tanks trust and reply quality.
- **Lowercase / conversational** often outperforms title-case "marketing" subjects.
- **Avoid spam triggers** — no ALL CAPS, no "free," no excessive punctuation, no "guarantee."
- Patterns that work: "{company} + {your company}?", "quick question about {their initiative}", "{mutual connection} suggested I reach out", "{specific trigger}".

## Personalization tiers

| Tier | Effort | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Deep** (1:1) | Researched trigger, custom angle per person | High-value accounts, founder-led |
| **Segment** | Tailored to a role/industry/trigger group | Scaled but relevant outreach |
| **Token** (name/company merge) | Minimal | Rarely enough on its own — avoid as the only personalization |

Default to the deepest tier the account value justifies. Token-only merge fields read as spam.

## Channel-specific notes

- **Email** — subject + first line are the whole game (preview pane). Keep under ~75–125 words. Plain text outperforms heavy HTML. One link max.
- **LinkedIn** — connection note (very short, ~300 char limit) or InMail. More personal; reference their content/role. (Deeper patterns in `linkedin-dms`.)
- **X/Twitter** — warm up first (engage with their content), then a short, casual DM. Relationship before pitch. (See `x-dms`.)
- **Direct response** — clear single offer and CTA; measure and iterate.

## Opener patterns that earn replies

- **Trigger-based**: "Saw {company} just {raised / launched / is hiring for X} — usually that means {relevant challenge}…"
- **Insight/observation**: "Noticed {specific thing about their site/product}. Most teams in your spot hit {problem}…"
- **Mutual connection**: "{Name} mentioned you're working on {X}…"
- **Genuine question**: "How are you currently handling {specific job}?"
- **Relevant proof**: "We helped {similar company} {specific result}. Wondering if {their} situation is similar."

Avoid: "I hope this email finds you well," "I'd love to pick your brain," "I know you're busy but…," paragraph-long company intros, and feature dumps.

## CTA patterns

- Interest-based: "Worth a quick look?" / "Want me to send the details?"
- Specific-but-light: "Open to a 15-min call next week?" (offer, don't demand)
- Value-add: "Happy to share the {relevant resource} either way."
Use **one** CTA. Multiple asks dilute and lower response.

## Quality gates

- **True and approved claims only** — no invented stats, customers, or capabilities; no fake "we met" / "we're connected" pretexts.
- **Real personalization** — the specific detail must be accurate; getting it wrong is worse than generic.
- **Compliant** — honor anti-spam norms (CAN-SPAM/GDPR): no deceptive subjects, include opt-out where required, no scraped-list blasting.
- **Volume in human-reviewable bounds** — no mass undifferentiated sends.
- **Human-gated** — present drafts and the target list; a human sends or enrolls.
- **Confidential** — treat prospect data as sensitive.

## Handoffs

- Multi-touch cadence around the first touch → `sequence`.
- After-touch replies/no-replies → `follow-up`.
- Channel-specific DM craft → `linkedin-dms`, `x-dms`.
- Account/persona research and angle → `prospect`.
