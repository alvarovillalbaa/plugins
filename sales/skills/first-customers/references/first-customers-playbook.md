# First Customers Playbook

Reference for first-customer selling: early wedge selection, founder-led sales, design partners, and first proof loops. The first ~10 customers are bought differently — you're selling vision and partnership, not a polished product, and learning as much as selling. Pull ICP/product facts from repo-local docs; outreach is human-gated.

## The first-customers mindset

- **Selling and learning are the same activity.** Every sales conversation is also discovery. The goal of early sales is signal (will people pay? for what? why?), not just revenue.
- **Founder-led by default.** Early customers buy from founders because only founders can sell the vision, make commitments, and turn feedback into product overnight. Don't hire a sales team to do this for you yet.
- **Do things that don't scale.** Manual onboarding, white-glove support, custom work — appropriate now, because the point is proof and learning, not efficiency.
- **Charge money early.** Paid pilots beat free ones — payment is the only real validation of value. Free users give polite feedback; paying ones give true signal.

## Choosing the wedge

The wedge = the narrow, acute problem you solve first, for a specific segment, that's the entry point to more.
- **Narrow beats broad.** "Best tool for X for Y-type companies" wins early; "platform for everything" wins nobody.
- **Pick acute pain** — a problem that's frequent, expensive, and currently solved badly (spreadsheets, manual work, a hated incumbent).
- **Pick a reachable, homogeneous segment** — so wins are referenceable to the next prospect and learnings compound.
- **Pick where you have an unfair advantage** — domain expertise, network, or insight.
- Test wedge fit: do prospects lean in immediately, or do you have to explain why they should care? Pull is the signal.

## Finding the first customers

- **Warm network first** — founders, ex-colleagues, advisors, investors' portfolios. Warm intros convert far better than cold at this stage.
- **Communities** where your ICP already gathers — be useful, not salesy.
- **Targeted cold outreach** to ideal-fit accounts (route to `outreach`/`initial` for drafting) — but keep volume low and personalization extreme.
- **Founder content / build-in-public** — attracts inbound from people with the problem.
- Quality over quantity: 20 perfect-fit conversations beat 500 sprayed emails.

## Design partners

A design partner is an early customer who co-builds with you in exchange for influence and favorable terms.
- **The deal**: they get early access, influence on the roadmap, and a great price/terms; you get deep feedback, a reference, and a committed user.
- **Pick partners who are**: in your ICP, feel the pain acutely, are willing to engage deeply (not just "interested"), and are referenceable.
- **Set expectations**: it's early, things will break, their feedback shapes the product — but you decide the roadmap (don't build a custom consultancy for one logo).
- **Aim for 3–5**, enough for patterns without diffusing focus.
- **Make it real** — a light agreement on access, feedback cadence, and (ideally) payment. Free design partners under-engage.

## Founder-led sales motion

1. **Prospect** narrowly (warm > cold). Route research to `prospect`.
2. **First conversation = discovery** — understand their world, their pain, how they solve it today, what it costs them. (Use `discovery` techniques: ask about past behavior, not hypotheticals.)
3. **Pitch the vision + the wedge** — connect their specific pain to your solution. Sell the outcome, not the feature list.
4. **Co-create the solution** — show, get reaction, iterate fast. Speed of response is your edge over incumbents.
5. **Ask for the money** — a paid pilot or contract. Price with conviction; discounting to zero teaches you nothing.
6. **Onboard manually** — white-glove, ensure first value, stay close.
7. **Capture the learning** — what resonated, what objections recurred, what they'd pay more for → feeds product and ICP.

## First proof loops

Build a tight loop: sell → deliver value → capture proof → use proof to sell the next one.
- **First value fast** — the faster a customer gets a win, the stronger your proof and the lower the churn.
- **Document outcomes** — even small wins become your first case studies and references.
- **Turn wins into references** — a referenceable first customer de-risks the second sale. Ask happy customers for intros and quotes.
- **Each win sharpens ICP** — who got the most value, fastest? That's your refined target. Feed to `system` ICP.

## Signals you have early traction

- Customers pay (real money, not free pilots).
- They use it repeatedly and expand usage unprompted.
- They'd be "very disappointed" without it (the PMF survey signal).
- They refer others without being asked.
- Sales conversations get easier as you reference earlier wins.
- Same pain + same buyer recurring across deals (a repeatable motion forming).

## Quality gates

- **No overpromising.** Selling vision is fine; fabricating current capabilities or fake customers is not. Be honest that it's early.
- **Don't build bespoke for one logo** — distinguish "feedback shaping the roadmap" from "custom consulting."
- **Charge.** Free-everything teaches nothing and attracts non-buyers.
- **Human-gated** — pilots, pricing, terms, and commitments require founder/owner sign-off and `finances` coordination.
- **Real outreach** — personalize from real signal; route drafting to `outreach`/`initial`.

## Handoffs

- Outreach drafting → `outreach`, `initial`, `sequence`.
- Account research → `prospect`.
- Discovery technique → product `discovery`.
- Expanding/retaining once landed → `customer-growth`.
- Wins into marketing assets → `marketing` (case studies); ICP refinement → `system` (`icp`).
