# LinkedIn DM Patterns

Reference for LinkedIn comments, connection requests, DM replies, follow-up sequences, and professional engagement writing. LinkedIn is relationship-first: warm the connection before you pitch. All outreach is human-gated; personalize from real signal.

## The LinkedIn relationship ladder

Cold pitch in a connection request is the lowest-converting move. Climb the ladder:
1. **Engage** — like and (better) comment thoughtfully on their posts.
2. **Connect** — send a personalized connection request (or none if already connected).
3. **Converse** — a relevant, no-ask first message once connected.
4. **Transition** — move to the value/ask only after rapport.

Skipping rungs ("connect → instant pitch") is the #1 reason LinkedIn outreach fails.

## Connection requests

- **Personalize when the reason is real**; a blank request is fine for clearly relevant peers, but a generic templated note is worse than nothing.
- **~300 character limit** — be brief: who you are + why connecting is relevant to *them*.
- **No pitch in the request.** The goal is the connection, not the deal.
- Patterns: shared context ("both working on X"), genuine interest in their work ("loved your post on Y"), mutual connection, relevant community.
- Avoid: "I'd like to add you to my network," immediate calendar links, feature dumps.

## Comments (engagement-led outreach)

Commenting is underrated top-of-funnel — it builds familiarity before any DM.
- **Add value** — a real insight, a relevant experience, a thoughtful question. Not "Great post! 🔥".
- **Be visible to their network** — good comments get seen by other prospects too.
- **Consistency** — showing up in someone's comments over time warms the eventual DM dramatically.
- Don't pitch in comments; build recognition.

## First DM (after connecting)

- **No-ask opener** is often best — reference their work/content, ask a genuine question, build rapport before any pitch.
- If leading with relevance: tie a specific trigger/their situation to a problem you understand, then a soft CTA.
- **Short** — LinkedIn DMs are read on mobile; a wall of text gets ignored.
- **Conversational, professional** — not a copy-pasted email pitch.
- **One soft ask** max, and only after some rapport.

## Follow-up cadence

Most replies come from follow-ups, but cadence must be respectful — LinkedIn is not email; over-DMing damages your reputation.

| Touch | Timing | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Day 0 | First DM (no-ask or soft relevance) |
| 2 | +4–7 days | Add value — a relevant resource, insight, or gentle nudge |
| 3 | +1–2 weeks | Different angle / new reason to talk |
| 4 | +2–3 weeks | Soft break-up ("I'll stop here — feel free to reach out if timing changes") |

Rules:
- **Space touches out** (days/weeks, not hours).
- **Each follow-up adds something** — a resource, a new angle, not "just bumping this."
- **Stop gracefully** after ~3–4 touches with no response; the break-up message often earns a reply.
- **Honor disinterest** immediately — a "no" or silence after the break-up ends the sequence.

## Reply handling

- **Positive** → move toward the next step (call/demo), keep momentum, make scheduling frictionless.
- **Objection** → acknowledge, address briefly, keep it conversational (don't dump a rebuttal).
- **Not now** → ask for a better time / permission to follow up later; nurture, don't push.
- **No** → thank them, leave the door open, disengage gracefully.

## Voice & quality

- Professional but human — first person, specific, warm. Run through `humanizing` to kill template tone.
- **Real personalization** — reference something true (their post, role, company trigger). Wrong details are worse than none.
- **No fabrication** — no fake mutual connections, fake "we met," invented results.
- Match the sender's voice doc.

## Quality gates

- **Relationship before pitch** — no instant-pitch connection requests.
- **Respectful cadence** — no spammy multi-DM-per-day; honor disinterest.
- **Human-gated** — present drafts and the sequence; a human sends/connects. No auto-DMing.
- **Compliant** — follow LinkedIn's norms; avoid automation that violates platform rules.
- **Confidential** — treat prospect data as sensitive.
